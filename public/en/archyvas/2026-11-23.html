Oil

On Friday Brent closed at 92.57 USD/bbl and ended the week lower for the third week in a row (EnergyNow; WSJ; Rigzone). In Sunday evening trading (2026-11-22) the price fell by 3.1% to 89.69 USD/bbl (81.61 €/bbl). The price is being weighed down by agencies' oversupply forecasts, recovering exports from the Middle East and an equity sell-off (OilPrice.com; EnergyNow; Reuters). The decline is limited by the Houthi attack on Saudi Arabia's Rabigh oil refinery and tanker incidents near the Strait of Hormuz (Reuters). The physical Brent premium to the futures contract on 2026-11-17 was −0.20 USD/bbl: this is a small discount and does not signal significant tension. Nevertheless, in one analyst's assessment, futures have decoupled from the strained Asian physical market (EnergyNow).

Gas and LNG

On Friday (2026-11-20) TTF rose by 4.7% to 88.45 €/MWh. This morning gas prices rose further on colder weather forecasts (Bloomberg), but there is no data on the new prices yet. An early breakdown of the polar vortex and weak wind could tighten the market further, as storage started the winter at its lowest level in more than a decade (CNBC). EU storage is 69.2% full (a year ago – 79.5%) and the Inčukalns storage facility is 52.7% full (a year ago – 59.0%). News on US gas is contradictory: according to data, the Henry Hub price rose by 6.2% to 3.62 USD/MMBtu, while EnergyNow reports a 4% fall to an 11-week low because of record production.

Impact on Lithuania

Oil and TTF are moving together (60-day correlation 0.66, β 0.82), but today the weather offsets the effect of cheaper oil on gas. Over 31 days the LT price was in the gas-fired power plant cost zone for 60.6% of the time, and in evening hours for 72.2%. When TTF rises by 10 €/MWh, the average LT price rises by ≈ 11.02 €/MWh. Today the evening peak price is 291.43 €/MWh, 98.0 €/MWh higher than the CCGT SRMC (193.4 €/MWh); it exceeds even the costs of a low-efficiency (η 40%) gas-fired power plant (264.8 €/MWh). The midday price is 241.05 €/MWh, so the BESS spread between the evening peak and midday is only 50.37 €/MWh, and solar power plants capture a high price at midday. The heating season may be more expensive than forecast, and the prime minister does not promise a quick fall in prices (LRT).
