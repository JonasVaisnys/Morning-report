Electricity market

Tuesday, 24 November · Lithuanian bidding zone · day-ahead market

Electricity today: 241.4 €/MWh – much more expensive than usual due to very expensive gas.

The price is pushed up most by very expensive gas (+86 €/MWh): TTF is at 91.4 €/MWh, 72% above its 12-month average. It is slightly pushed down by strong wind (−11 €/MWh): wind farms are generating 685 MW – 39% of consumption (1,772 MW); normal for the time of year is about 513 MW.

Exports to Poland use almost the full interconnector capacity (LitPol – 212 of 222 MW). For 11 hours the interconnectors from Latvia are fully loaded, so electricity in Lithuania is more expensive than in Latvia, by 31.3 €/MWh on average over the day.

Oil

The Brent futures contract closed on Monday at 86.53 USD/bbl: down 6.5% on the day and 25.9% over 30 days. The price was pushed down by signs of US–Iran diplomacy and D. Trump's hints that more time would be given to negotiations (Bloomberg; Rigzone). A possible agreement between Iran and the Persian Gulf states on the Strait of Hormuz (EnergyNow) and a global supply surplus (WSJ) also contributed. On Tuesday morning the price stabilised (Bloomberg). One report, by contrast, states that oil rose by more than 1% and is recording its strongest month since March (EnergyNow), but this does not match other sources and data. The physical Brent premium to the futures contract on 11-17 was −0.20 USD/bbl, so there is no large premium. At the same time the Asian physical market is weakening and detaching from futures (EnergyNow), while the diesel shortage persists (Reuters).

Gas and LNG

TTF rose on Monday to 91.39 €/MWh: +3.3% on the day and +7.8% over 7 days. The price was driven up by forecast cold weather and fear of a supply shortage (The Guardian). Gas did not follow oil this time, although the 60-day correlation between them is 0.60 (β 0.72): the price is now determined more by weather and inventories. EU storage is 68.9% full (a year ago – 79.1%) and Inčukalns storage is 51.8% full (a year ago – 58.6%). Norwegian gas production reached a 9-month high in October (Rigzone), while US LNG exporters are struggling to find buyers in Europe for new supply (EnergyNow).
